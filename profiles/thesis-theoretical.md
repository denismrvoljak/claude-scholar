# Profile: theoretical or literature-based thesis

A thesis whose evidence is other people's work: a systematic or structured literature review, a conceptual paper, a comparative analysis of theories, or a legal, historical, or philosophical argument. No primary data collection.

The defining difficulty is that the method is invisible unless you make it visible. An empirical thesis proves it did something by describing its data. A literature-based thesis has to prove it by describing its search and selection, or the examiner cannot distinguish scholarship from a well-read opinion.

## Structure

| # | Chapter | Does what | Budget share |
|---|---------|-----------|-------------:|
| 1 | Introduction | The problem, why it needs a conceptual rather than empirical treatment, the research questions, the delimitation. | 10% |
| 2 | Method | How the literature was searched, screened, and selected. Databases, queries, dates, inclusion and exclusion criteria, and how many works survived each stage. In a review, this chapter *is* the method. | 15% |
| 3 | Conceptual foundations | The concepts and traditions the analysis works with, defined precisely enough to be used. | 20% |
| 4 | Analysis | The synthesis: where the literature agrees, where it conflicts, what it assumes, and what it has not addressed. Organised by theme, never by paper. | 30% |
| 5 | Discussion | What follows. The framework, typology, research agenda, or position the thesis contributes. | 15% |
| 6 | Conclusion | Answers, contributions, limits, future work. | 10% |

## What examiners look for

**A reproducible search.** Exact queries, databases, date ranges, and screening counts. A flow diagram from records identified to studies included is standard in systematic reviews and helps in any structured one.

**Synthesis rather than summary.** A chapter organised as "Smith found… Jones found… Patel found…" is an annotated bibliography. Organise by theme, by mechanism, or by disagreement, and let each source appear wherever it bears on the question.

**Engagement with disagreement.** A review that finds unanimity has usually not read widely enough. Conflicting findings, and an account of what explains the conflict, are the most valuable thing this genre produces.

**Explicit criteria.** Why these sources and not others? Why this date range? Why exclude non-English work, and what that costs?

**A contribution of its own.** A framework, a typology, a reframing, a research agenda. A thesis that only reports what others said has not made an argument.

## Common failures

The search protocol described so loosely that no one could repeat it. Chronological organisation, which produces a history rather than an analysis. Every source treated as equally authoritative regardless of method or sample. No account of publication bias or of the literature's own blind spots. A conclusion that restates the summary.

## Onboarding questions specific to this profile

- Systematic, semi-systematic, or narrative? The answer sets how much of the method chapter has to be protocol.
- Which databases, and is institutional access available?
- Is a PRISMA-style flow required or expected?
- How were inclusion and exclusion criteria set, and when — before the search or after seeing the results?
- What is the intended contribution beyond synthesis?
- Roughly how many sources are expected? The number changes how the analysis chapter must be organised.
