## Section order

The order in `paper.md` is the order of the document. Where the institution mandates a structure, that structure wins over any default.

| # | Section | File | Budget |
|---|---------|------|-------:|
| 0 | Front matter | `sections/front_matter.md` | excluded |
| 1 | <Introduction> | `sections/introduction.md` | <chars> |
| 2 | <...> | | |
| n | References | `sections/references.md` | excluded |

**Total budget: <N> characters**, against a limit of <N> <characters including spaces / words / pages>.

## Counting rules

<From docs/requirements.md. State exactly what is excluded, and how figures and tables count. For example: front page, table of contents, bibliography, and appendices are excluded; a figure pasted as an image counts 800 characters regardless of size; a table typed as text counts its actual characters.>

Reserve budget for figures before drafting. A document with ten pasted figures under a rule of 800 characters each has 8,000 characters less for prose than its section budgets suggest.

## Front matter

<Title page, blank page, abstract, table of contents, list of abbreviations, list of figures, list of tables — whichever the institution requires, in its required order.>

## Cross-referencing

- Sections, figures, and tables by number.
- Figures and tables numbered consecutively across the whole document.
- Every figure and table is referred to at least once in the prose. One that is never mentioned should not be there.
- Forward references are allowed, briefly, and only where the target delivers.

## Consistency across sections

Terminology and notation introduced anywhere apply everywhere. Symbols are defined on first use and listed in the notation section if the document has one. A concept renamed halfway through reads to an examiner as two concepts confused.
